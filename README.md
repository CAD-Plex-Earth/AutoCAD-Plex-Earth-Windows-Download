## 🧠 Overview

**Plex-Earth** enhances CAD-based design by bringing geographic information into the same workspace used for drafting and modeling. The add-on is designed for projects that require a connection between digital drawings and real-world locations, providing access to imagery, terrain information, elevation data, and other spatial references.

Its toolkit supports a variety of geospatial tasks, including positioning drawings geographically, incorporating aerial imagery, creating terrain representations, working with contours, and examining elevation changes. These capabilities allow users to study existing site conditions and add meaningful geographic context to conventional CAD geometry.

**Plex-Earth** can be applied to architecture, civil engineering, infrastructure, construction, surveying, environmental planning, and site development. By combining mapping resources with familiar CAD tools, it gives users a practical way to visualize project locations, investigate terrain characteristics, and develop designs with greater awareness of their surrounding environment.

## Additional Information for Windows Users

Plex-Earth creates a bridge between traditional CAD drafting and location-based project information. Instead of treating a drawing as an isolated collection of geometry, users can place it within its actual geographic setting and work with supporting imagery and terrain information directly alongside the design.

The terrain workflow is useful when the shape and elevation of the ground are important to a project. Elevation data can be transformed into visual terrain elements, while contour information and imagery provide additional references for understanding the characteristics of a site and planning around its natural features.

For Windows-based CAD workflows, **Plex-Earth** provides a focused collection of mapping and terrain capabilities that can complement architectural, engineering, surveying, and construction projects. Its geospatial tools help users investigate locations, visualize surrounding conditions, organize spatial information, and incorporate real-world context into their designs.
